As civilization reaches toward the Moon, Mars, orbital habitats, and eventually deeper into space,
entirely new challenges will emerge — challenges no one has solved yet.
Beyond 2030 is your chance to find one of those future problems and build a real technological
solution for it.
We’re not handing you a specific problem to solve. You get to decide everything:

PART II — THE BUILD
Every project should pass three simple questions:
Why will this problem exist? What future development creates it?
Why does it matter? Who gets hurt, and what happens if nobody solves it?
Why is your solution the right one? Why does it actually address the problem?
You don’t need to prove your prediction will come true. You need to show that your scenario is
coherent, well-reasoned, and technically grounded.
Use whatever technology helps your solution — AI/ML, IoT, cybersecurity, robotics, cloud, data
engineering, computer vision, simulation, AR/VR, blockchain, distributed systems, digital twins,
embedded systems, or anything else that makes sense.
Technology should serve your problem — not the other way around.
Adding a technology just to make your project look more advanced doesn’t earnextra points. A
simple solution that nails a hard problem can easily beat a complex one that doesn’t actually solve
anything.
For every major technology in your stack, ask yourself:
Actually work — not just look good
Show a clear connection between the problem and your solution
Have a core feature that works end to end in the live demo
Come with a clear explanation of how it’s built
Your solution should lean toward a SaaS-style product philosophy — a usable product or service built
for defined users, not a one-off script — but we’re not restricting your architecture. It could be a
web platform, cloud service, mobile app, desktop app, IoT system, simulation, or anything else — as
long as it tackles the challenge in a meaningful way.

Being able to generate code is not the same as understanding the system that code
creates.
This challenge isn’t about who can write the most code. Modern tools — especially AI — have made
building sophisticated software more accessible than ever. And that’s the point: as implementation
gets easier, your ability to find meaningful problems and imagine valuable solutions matters even
more.
We put serious weight on creativity, problem selection, future thinking, product design, user
experience, and your understanding of what you built.
A technically impressive product solving a tiny problem shouldn’t automatically
outscore a simple, brilliantly original solution tackling a major future challenge.

PART IV — EVALUATION
13 How You’ll Be Scored
13.3 Future Relevance — 20 pts
Judges look at: how plausible your futurescenario is, how it connects to humanity’s expansion beyond
Earth, your understanding of future tech or societal shifts, the strength of your reasoning,
and how well you explain why this problem will matter.
You don’t need to be a fortune teller — you need to build a convincing case for the future
you’ve chosen.
13.4 Problem Significance — 10 pts
Judges look at: how severe the problem is, how many or how important the affected people are,
what the consequences of leaving it unsolved would be, whether current solutions exist, and how
relevant it is to life beyond Earth.
13.2 SaaS / Solution Quality — 30 pts
This is the biggest category — it’s all about thequality of what you actually built:
13.1 Innovation & Creativity — 25 pts
Judges look at: how original your concept is, how creative your problem is, the novelty of your
approach, how well you interpreted the challenge, creative use of technology, how your solution
stands apart from what already exists, and whether it could change how people think about the
problem.
The most innovative solution isn’t always the most technically complex one.
Functionality — does it work?
Effectiveness — does it really solve the problem you defined?
Technical implementation — is it coherent and well-chosen?
UX/UI — is it intuitive, usable, and thoughtfully designed?
Reliability — does it hold up during the live demo?
Product coherence — do the features all serve the mission?
Execution — how well did you turn your idea into reality?
A pretty interface can’t save a product that doesn’t solve its problem. And
impressive engineering can’t save something that’s impossible to use.

A problem doesn’t have to affect millions to be significant. A specialized problem
affecting astronauts, researchers, or critical infrastructure can carry enormous
weight.
Be ready to explain WHY your system works — not just show THAT it works.AsAI

18 The Spirit of Beyond 2030
The future doesn’t need more technologyfor the sake of technology. It needs people who know
what technology should be built.
17 What Wins, and What Doesn’t
There’s no magic formula. A winning project might be technically sophisticated or remarkably
simple. AI-powered or completely AI-free. IoT-heavy or built on a single clever algorithm. Focused on
UX, infrastructure, or a human problem nobody else noticed.
What makes a great project? When the jury thinks: “I never thought about this
problem that way” — and then: “and they actually built something that could solve
it.
”
Yourproject won’t score higher just because it:
Uses AI
Uses many technologies
Has a massive codebase
Has complicated architecture
Uses expensive cloud infrastructure
Looks futuristic
PART VI — CLOSING
Complexity is not innovation. Code volume is not impact. Technology is not the idea.
AI can now help you write code, generate interfaces, build prototypes, and accelerate development.
But no tool will ever automatically answer the questions that matter most:
Beyond 2030 puts those questions at the center. You’re not competing to build the most complex
app — you’re competing to show that you can imagine, question, identify, design, build, and defend.
The challenge starts before the first line of code.
Humanity has always expanded by solving problems it had never faced before. The next frontier will
be no different. As we leave Earth, new environments will create new constraints, new systems, new
opportunities, and entirely new problems — some of which seem impossible today, and some of
which don’t even exist yet.
Your mission: find one. Then build the solution.
May the code be with you.
What should we build? Why? Who needs it? What future does it create?
